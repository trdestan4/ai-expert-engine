# Originality Evidence

Originality is evidenced by product-specific design logic, not novelty claims or uncommon decoration.

## Strong evidence

Look for several independent signals:
- composition derived from actual content/product structure;
- a category convention intentionally retained/challenged with rationale;
- distinctive brand asset transformed into a repeatable system;
- typography behavior tied to brand/content needs;
- imagery/material language tied to product truth;
- meaningful signature interaction or spatial behavior;
- unusual but credible content sequencing;
- responsive recomposition preserving the same idea;
- details/states carrying the visual language beyond the hero;
- design remaining recognizable when logo is hidden.

## Swap test

Temporarily replace logo/name/product imagery with a competitor. If the design remains essentially identical, differentiation is weak. Then inspect which layers are generic: composition, type, color roles, media, content sequence, motion, UI states.

## Category-code test

Originality does not require rejecting every convention. A checkout, medical form or banking confirmation may need strong convention for trust. The question is whether non-critical layers create distinct recognition without harming comprehension.

## Trend half-life

Flag devices whose distinctiveness depends entirely on current trend novelty: glassmorphism, aurora gradients, bento grids, chrome 3D type, oversized pill buttons, floating blobs, universal grain, device mockups. They can be used, but only if product/brand logic survives after the trend label is removed.

## System depth

Test at least: hero, information-heavy section, comparison/table/list, form/state, footer/utility surface and mobile. A signature that only appears once is decoration; a signature repeated mechanically everywhere becomes gimmick. Good systems have controlled recurrence and contrast.

## Scoring

Score 0–2 with evidence on: product-specific composition, brand-specific grammar, typography rationale, color rationale, imagery coherence, content/narrative specificity, motion purpose, responsive originality without usability loss. 13–16 pass; 10–12 pass with notes; 6–9 revise; 0–5 reject. Do not let the total hide a zero on a critical dimension.

Weak evidence: unusual hex values, random gradients, trendy 3D, special radius, different font on a standard template, or “nobody else uses this.”
