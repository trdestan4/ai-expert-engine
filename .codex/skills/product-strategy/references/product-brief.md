# Product Brief Reference

Use this only when a structured product brief improves downstream decisions.

## Minimum fields

- **Outcome:** observable business/user result.
- **Primary audience:** who arrives and in what decision context.
- **Primary job:** the most important thing the user must accomplish.
- **Value proposition:** promise, supporting benefit, evidence, differentiation.
- **Trust requirements:** proof needed before action.
- **Core scope:** capabilities required for the primary outcome.
- **Supporting scope:** useful but secondary capabilities.
- **Constraints:** business, content, legal, operational, platform, performance.
- **Experience principles:** 3–6 project-specific rules.
- **Success evidence:** metrics or observable behavior.
- **Assumptions:** only items that could change later decisions.

## Quality rules

A strong brief is shorter than the project conversation. Remove biography, market trivia, and feature ideas that do not change a product/design/engineering decision.

Avoid fictional personas unless behavior materially differs. Prefer intent/context such as “repeat buyer comparing replacement parts from mobile” over invented age/job profiles.

Treat words like premium, modern, trustworthy, innovative, simple, fast, exclusive, and luxurious as unresolved design criteria. Translate them into behavior and proof before passing them to creative specialists.

## Example translation

Input: “Premium dental clinic site.”

Weak interpretation: dark navy + gold, serif headings, glass cards.

Useful product interpretation: high-consideration health service; visitors need confidence in clinicians, treatment clarity, evidence, transparent next steps, calm appointment flow, and high-quality clinical imagery without visual coldness.
