# Visual Art Direction System

Create a visual bible only for media the product actually needs. Art direction defines evidence, atmosphere and recognition across a set; it is not a list of image prompts.

## Asset jobs

Classify each major asset: evidence, product truth, atmosphere, explanation, category recognition, social proof, editorial narrative, brand signature or decoration. Evidence-critical assets have stricter accuracy/provenance rules than atmospheric imagery.

## Define the visual grammar

- dominant medium and supporting media;
- realism/stylization level;
- subject casting/product geometry requirements;
- lighting direction, hardness, color temperature and contrast;
- camera/lens/perspective, distance and depth-of-field behavior;
- crop, focal point, scale, negative space and intended text-safe areas;
- background/environment and material language;
- texture/finish/grain and post-processing;
- color relationship with UI surfaces;
- icon/illustration geometry and stroke/fill rules;
- mobile/alternate crop rules.

## Photography

Specify authenticity level, location/set, wardrobe/props, expressions/actions, product handling and diversity relevant to real audience. Avoid generic stock gestures and cinematic haze unrelated to brand. For clinical/industrial/legal/ecommerce evidence, truth and clarity outrank mood.

## 3D and rendering

Define physically plausible materials, scale, edge treatment, light/reflection and camera consistency. Decide whether 3D explains a product/process or merely decorates. Heavy WebGL/interactive 3D must justify performance/accessibility cost; pre-rendered sequences may deliver the same narrative more safely.

## AI-generated assets

Lock subject/product geometry where truth matters. Maintain character/product identity, lens/perspective, lighting, environment and post-process across a set. Reject fake text, malformed logos, plastic materials, impossible reflections/shadows and inconsistent anatomy/scale. Track prompt/model/source provenance when production governance requires it.

Use source photography/renders for evidence-critical product views when generation cannot guarantee accuracy. Never imply a generated depiction is documentary proof.

## Illustration/iconography

Define grid, perspective, stroke, corner language, filled/outline mixing, detail level and color behavior. Icons must support recognition at real sizes; do not turn every feature into a decorative icon tile.

## Production and delivery

Specify responsive aspect ratios, art-directed crops, focal preservation, transparent vs baked backgrounds, format/compression, DPR needs, dark/light adaptation, alt/caption requirements and performance budget. Asset choices coordinate with `asset-production` and frontend implementation.

## Review

Evaluate a set, not one hero image. Test evidence sections, repetitive cards/listings, mobile, dark mode, empty/loading states and long pages. The visual language should remain coherent without every image looking identical.
