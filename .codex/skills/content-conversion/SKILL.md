# Purpose

Turn product truth into clear, differentiated and persuasive communication that helps the right user understand value, reduce uncertainty and take the next useful action without manipulation.

## Use when

- landing-page, homepage, service/product-page, onboarding, form, CTA or microcopy needs design;
- value proposition, messaging hierarchy, proof, objections or conversion friction is unclear;
- copy must align with brand voice and measurable user intent;
- an existing page converts poorly and message/decision friction may be the cause.

## Do not use when

- keyword/indexation strategy is primary (`seo`);
- visual hierarchy/layout is primary (`ux-ui-design`);
- pricing/business-model design is primary (`product-strategy`, `ecommerce`, `saas-platform`);
- deceptive urgency, forced continuity or hidden-cost patterns are requested.

## Inputs

Establish audience segment, job-to-be-done, awareness stage, product truth, differentiators, alternatives, objections, proof, offer, next action, brand voice, legal/product constraints and available conversion evidence.

## Workflow

### 1. Clarify the decision
Define what the user must understand, believe and do next. Separate primary conversion from secondary exploration.

### 2. Build message hierarchy
Lead with specific outcome/context, then differentiation, mechanism, proof, objection handling, offer details and CTA. Do not front-load empty adjectives.

### 3. Write from evidence
Use product facts, customer language, operational proof, case evidence and meaningful specifics. Avoid unsupported superlatives and fabricated social proof.

### 4. Remove friction
Make pricing conditions, form requirements, errors, next steps, delivery expectations and cancellation/commitment implications understandable before action.

### 5. Optimize ethically
Use urgency/scarcity only when true. Avoid preselected consent, disguised ads, confirm-shaming, hidden subscription terms or obstructive cancellation.

### 6. Test material hypotheses
For meaningful traffic, define measurable variants around proposition, proof, offer framing or CTA; do not A/B test random synonyms without a behavioral hypothesis.

## Decision rules

- Clarity beats cleverness at decision points.
- Specific proof beats generic prestige language.
- One page may support multiple intents, but one primary action should dominate each decision state.
- Copy must match actual product capability and commercial terms.
- Reduce anxiety before asking for commitment.
- Conversion optimization cannot override accessibility, privacy, security or truthful disclosure.

## Quality gates

- Target user and decision are explicit.
- Headline/value proposition communicates concrete relevance.
- Claims are supportable.
- CTA describes a sensible next action.
- Pricing/commitment/important constraints are not hidden.
- Forms/errors/confirmation copy reduce ambiguity.
- No dark-pattern or fake-proof tactics.

## Failure handling

If product differentiation is unknown, do not invent it; surface the gap and write a truthful temporary message. If conversion data is absent, distinguish heuristic diagnosis from measured evidence.

## Output contract

Return audience/decision model, message hierarchy, final copy/microcopy, proof/objection plan, ethical conversion opportunities, experiment hypothesis when relevant, and cross-skill handoffs.
