# Value Proposition and Messaging

Messaging should help a specific audience understand relevance, value, proof and next step. It is not a collection of clever slogans.

## Message architecture

Build from: audience/situation → problem/job → promised progress → mechanism/differentiation → proof → objection handling → action. For complex products, separate company-level proposition from product/use-case/page-level messages.

A strong proposition answers: who is this for, what meaningful outcome changes, why this approach is credible/different and what evidence reduces risk. Avoid “innovative, seamless, cutting-edge, premium” unless the page immediately explains what those words mean operationally.

## Research inputs

Use customer/sales/support language, search queries, reviews, win/loss notes, objections, product usage and competitor claims. Distinguish customer language worth mirroring from misconceptions that need clearer education.

For high-consideration products, map objections by stage: “is this relevant?”, “will it work for me?”, “can I trust you?”, “what will it cost/require?”, “what happens if it fails?” Proof should appear near the objection it resolves rather than in one generic testimonial section.

## Proof hierarchy

Prefer specific evidence: product demonstration, quantified outcome with context, methodology, named customer/case, credentials, guarantees/reversibility, operational facts, transparent pricing/process. Generic adjectives and anonymous quotes are weak proof.

Never fabricate scarcity, testimonials, logos, ratings or metrics. If evidence is unavailable, write a truthful placeholder/request rather than inventing trust.

## Differentiation

Differentiate on something the user values and the company can defend: workflow, speed to value, data/control, expertise, service model, integration, quality evidence, economics or experience. Being visually different is not product differentiation.

## Message testing

Check five-second comprehension, jargon, specificity, claim-to-proof distance, mobile truncation, localization and whether CTA reflects the user's readiness. A clear “See treatment options” can outperform an aggressive “Book now” when trust is not yet established.

## Handoff

Provide message hierarchy, proof requirements, claims needing verification, objection map, CTA strategy and alternate copy for testing—not just a hero headline.
