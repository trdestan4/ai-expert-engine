# Brand System Reference

A digital identity is a recognition system, not a logo file. Choose the few signals that can remain distinctive across marketing pages, products, dashboards, email, social and small UI states.

## Strategy to identity

Translate positioning and personality into observable design behaviors. “Confident” may mean decisive hierarchy and controlled contrast; “calm clinical authority” may mean precise evidence framing, restrained motion and neutral-warm surfaces. Avoid mapping adjectives directly to cliché palettes/fonts.

## Identity primitives

Possible primitives: mark/wordmark, color signature, typography behavior, graphic geometry, framing/crop, image treatment, icon/illustration style, layout rhythm, material/texture, motion and verbal cues. Do not require all of them.

Define which are **core recognition assets**, **supporting assets**, and **contextual expressions**. Core signals should survive monochrome, dark/light and reduced-motion situations.

## Logo/mark checks

- concept has a reason beyond category symbol;
- recognizable at favicon/small size;
- legible in monochrome and single-color reproduction;
- not dependent on gradient/shadow/effect;
- clear space/minimum size are practical, not ceremonial;
- horizontal/stacked/compact behavior exists when needed;
- symbol/wordmark relationship remains balanced across responsive contexts;
- legal/trademark similarity risk is flagged for human/legal review rather than guessed away.

Avoid generic tooth/house/truck/brain/bolt symbols unless transformed into a genuinely ownable concept.

## Typography and color as brand

Specify behavior, not only names: headline casing, line-break style, numeral treatment, editorial vs UI voice, accent frequency, surface hierarchy, dark mode adaptation. A brand that disappears when the logo is hidden is over-dependent on the mark.

## Imagery and graphic language

Define subject matter, realism, lighting, camera/perspective, crop, background, material and treatment. Separate evidence photography from atmospheric imagery. For AI assets, lock geometry/subject truth when accuracy matters and define consistency rules across a set.

## Voice and naming

When verbal identity is in scope, define tone axes (e.g. expert↔casual, concise↔narrative, warm↔clinical) plus examples of what the brand does/does not sound like. Avoid generic “friendly, professional, innovative.” Product terminology and CTA verbs should be consistent.

## Brand architecture

For product families/subbrands, decide endorsed, monolithic or independent relationships based on trust transfer, audience overlap and operational complexity. Do not invent subbrands merely to decorate product tiers.

## Digital stress tests

Hide the logo and test long pages, dense ecommerce/catalog screens, authenticated product UI, mobile, error/empty states, charts/tables, reduced color and real translations. Identity must remain coherent without making every component decorative.

## Governance

Document token/source-of-truth ownership, approved variants, asset export rules and exceptions. A living design system should allow evolution without breaking recognition; record why a signature exists so later teams do not “simplify” it into generic defaults.
