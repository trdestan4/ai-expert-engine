# Design Quality Policy

Applies to all creative/design skills.

## Non-negotiables

1. Never map prestige/style adjectives directly to stock aesthetics.
2. Product/audience/content context precedes visual style.
3. Familiar UX conventions may remain familiar; originality belongs where it adds brand/product value.
4. Design must work beyond the hero: long content, mobile, edge states, real assets, light/dark when required.
5. Accessibility and performance are design constraints from the start.
6. Effects require purpose: hierarchy, feedback, spatial continuity, narrative, recognition, or atmosphere tied to the product.
7. Avoid trend stacking.
8. Real content/product evidence outranks decorative filler.
9. Design systems should produce consistent variation, not identical cards everywhere.
10. A major creative direction must pass `anti-generic-design` before approval.

## Prestige-adjective guard

Words including premium, luxury, elegant, modern, futuristic, corporate, innovative, minimalist, and exclusive are unresolved requirements. Translate them into product-specific criteria before selecting palette, fonts, imagery, geometry, or motion.

## Acceptance

A design direction is acceptable only when its major choices can be explained from product, audience, brand, content, or interaction needs and remain feasible across responsive/accessibility/performance constraints.
